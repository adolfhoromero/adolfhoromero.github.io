“Trike Is No Treat: Health Problems at Monarch”

[c. 1990–1991]Approximate date

A survey by UAW Local 802's new health and safety committee at Monarch Machine Tool in Cortland found many workers reporting symptoms linked to trichloroethane (“Trike”), a degreasing solvent. The article explains its hazards and the committee's next steps.

“For a machine shop it’s pretty clean.”
That’s one reaction many workers have to
working conditions at Monarch Machine
Tool in Cortland.

Probe a little further, however, and you
find that things may not be so rosy. A
‘number of the UAW Local 802 members who
use or come in contact with trichloroethane, a
cleaning solvent, have problems with their
eyes; some don’t sleep well; others find their
noses frequently stuffy at work. Are they
being slowly poisoned by the Trike?

A few months ago, Local President Ron
Powell passed around a Workplace Health
Survey. He was surprised and disturbed at
the results: :

The National Institute for Occupational
Safety and Health (NIOSH, a government
research agency) recommends that Trike be
handled with extreme caution “limiting
exposure to as few employees as possible and
reducing exposures to the lowest possible
limits.”

Monarch’s management has not complied
with this recommendation. Nor do they
conduct air sampling to see what level of
Trike workers are being exposed to. Nor do
they provide a good local exhaust ventilation
for worksites where Trike is used. Yet the
smell of Trike is regularly in the air at
Monarch. This is a clear warning of
hazardous exposure. If workers. are
smelling it there is a real fear that air levels
may be exceeding the danger limit, which
NIOSH sets at 350 parts per million, not to be
exceeded during any 15 minute period.

At its meeting February 3, Local 802’s
newly formed Union Health and Safety
Committee began considering what it can do
in this matter. One option is to find safer
alternatives and force management to use
them. Since the Trike is so expensive to
dispose of—because it is so hazardous—
Monarch does have an interest in alter-
natives (too bad they aren’t as concerned
about workers’ health as about the costs of
disposal). The Committee also considered

the need to do air testing, and to assist the
most affected workers in having a medical
Dr. Michael Lax, Director of
the new Occupational Health Clinic at
Syracuse Medical Center, would be the best
person to do this.
