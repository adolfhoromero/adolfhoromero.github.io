Note 1 - Summary of Significant Accounting Policies
Activity

The Labor Council is an affiliated and charte

eu red union with the American Federation of Labor and Congress
of Industrial Organizations (AFL-CIO

). The Labor Council is organized as an unincorporated association and

operated as a not-for-profit organization to support local member unions through educational and informational

programs. Membership in the Labor Council is open to affiliated local unions, councils, joint boards, district
councils, and other organizations supporting the goals of the Labor Council.

Basis of Accounting

The Labor Council's policy is to prepare its financial statements on the cash basis of accounting;
consequently, revenues are recognized when received rather than when earned, and expenses are
recognized when cash is disbursed rather than when the obligation is incurred.

Fund Accounting

To ensure observance of limitations and restrictions placed on the use of resources available to the Labor
Council, the accounts of the Council are maintained in accordance with the principles of fund accounting. That

is the procedure by which resources for various purposes are classified for accounting and reporting purposes

into funds established according to their nature and purposes. Separate accounts are maintained for each
fund.

The assets, liabilities, and net assets of the Council are reported in three self-balancing fund groups as
follows:

The General Fund, also known as the Operating Fund, represents the portion of expendable funds that are
available for support of the various unions.

The Health & Safety Fund accounts for the New York State Department of Labor Grant.

The Cortland Area Workers Rights Fund is a spin-off organization supporting union workers in Cortland
County.

Note 2 - Related Party Transactions

; The Labor Council leases office space under an operating lease from a member, the UAW Local 2300, for

387.50 per month. The Council receives $ 137.50 per month from the AFL-CIO for use of a portion of the
space.

The Labor Council also shares a copier, copier supplies, a postage meter, a fax telephone line, a drinking
fountain and bookkeeping services. The Council shares the cost of some of these expenses with the UAW and
the Council receives donated services/supplies for some of these items.

Note 3 - Subsequent Events

The Labor Council was placed into trusteeship by the President of the AFL-CIO on January 27, 1999. The
Council is currently being managed by the appointed Trustee, Roger Clayman, AFL-CIO Field Representative.

Note 4 - 1997 Restated Financial Statements

The 1997 Financial Statements have been restated to include the Cortland Area Workers Rights Board and
the payroll tax liabilities have been eliminated to reflect the cash basis of accounting.

Note 5 - Concentrations

The Labor Council receives its primary support from the New York State Department of Labor and the City of
Ithaca. The Department of Labor has conducted an annual audit of their contract with the Council.
