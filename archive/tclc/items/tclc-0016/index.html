March 19, 1993

To: The Steering Committee

RE: AFL-CIO Affiliation Process/Bylaws

March 19, 1993
To All:

Here are some thoughts on the AFL-CIO process:

You essentially have two routes to follow if the Steering Committee still
wishes AFL affiliation. These are either a)To create a parralel organization with
bylaws nearly identical to the Labor Coalition but which would be incorporated
within it; or b) to wage a fight with the AFL to have our first draft constitution
approved with whatever revisions they might require.

The first option would procede by first drafting a set of "Labor Council’
bylaws, which is essentially where the conversations with Leonard Lewis are
leading. This can probably be achieved by mid-april.

It will then be necessary to take this draft 'Labor Council’ constitution

and modify it more along the lines of the previous draft approved by the
steering committee. Here is some ideas for modifications:

a) incorporate the members of the ‘labor council’ board in its entirety to
the ‘coalition’ steering committee. Add two members for a the Labor Coalition,
and poof! you have a Steering Committee in one election at one meeting. You can
make a policy decision on matters like having only AFL unions vote on ‘Labor
Council’ members, do it all at once, etc.

b) Change the membership article to include language allowing
independants, individual members and perhaps local community organizations.

c) Names: i.e. Steering Committee vs. Executive Board, Co-Chairs vs Vice
Presidents, etc.

d) Change dues article to refer to the newly allowed organizations.

That's about it! After approving the ‘labor Council’ bylaws in their
enterity, and approving the new articles in the Labor Coalition bylaws, you may
then through the Labor Council bylaws away and only refer to the Labor
Coalition Bylaws, except in special cases as preferred by the Steering Committee
where it's deemed expedient to have only the 'Labor Council' make a decision
of some sort. It sounds more complicated than it realy is. :

The other route is to circumvent Lewis and complain to Cole and Crayton
that they are reneging on their agreements to us concerning the impact of
affiliation, and claim that our proposed original draft constitution complies with
the AFL-CIO's. This will be a fight and may become complicated and nasty, but

the steering Committee may want to pursue this route if there is concern over
the structure and cohesion of the organization.
