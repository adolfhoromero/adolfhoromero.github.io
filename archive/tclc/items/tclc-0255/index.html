Health and Safety Program annual report, 1990–1991

[1991]Date inferred

The program's year: back injury prevention clinics with CSEA Local 605, the Ithaca school district employees, APWU Local 1151, and UAW Local 2300; work with union health and safety committees at Monarch Machine Tool, Pall Trinity, and Brewer-Titchener; workshops and conferences; the FYI newsletter for office workers; information and referral; the first Workers Memorial Day events after Charlie Streeter's death on the job; and help for Ithaca College office workers made ill at work.

Back Injury Prevention Clinics

Back injury prevention clinics were organized with CSEA 605,
the Ithaca School District Employees Association, APWU 1151 and
UAW 2300. In each case, a walkthrough took place prior to the
training; a report with recommendations for change was prepared
for the union; a physical therapist conducted a back clinic. In
three of the four cases, the clinic took place on company time.
A brief summary of each follows.

general stores at SUNY-Cortland. Twenty workers attended the
clinic. A series of recommendations was provided to the union.

ithaca College

A major unplanned activity during the year was our efforts to
train and assist office workers at a non-union workplace (ithaca
Gollege). Early in the year we were contacted for assistance by
four College employees, all of whom had contracted Multiple
Chemical Sensitivity |!]]ness at work (three from one building)
and were disabled. The College refused to acknowledge any
problem or that the illnesses were work-related.

The workers asked for assistance to fulfill two objectives:
1) to achieve a safe workplace for those workers stil. at work;

and 2) to pressure the College to provide the disabled workers
with their due compensation.

During the course of this year-long and ongoing campaign we:

held numerous meetings with the IC workers;

helped train the workers to use the media;

helped the workers draft and distribute an

informational
letter to all IC employees;

helped the workes draft a formal complaint to OSHA and
to mediate with OSHA;

helped identify and elicit other sources of assistance
and legal support, including the NLRB.
