Since 1985 the Tompkins/Cortland Labor Coalition has made public its
distress over the plant closings and large workforce reductions which have
plagued this country and this region. Locally, this trend appears to have
abated and in some small fashion reversed itself. Some new manufacturing
jobs have opened up and for that we are all quite happy. Still, concern
exists in our community over the potential of further loss of good paying
jobs and the need for advance notification of major fluctuations in
employment.

We are pleased to enclose a copy of the new federal Plant Closing Law,
called the "Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act." Also en-
closed you will find a brief sheet highlighting the law, entitled “Highlights
of the 1988 Plant Closing Law."

While the federal law is a beginning, we do not feel that it fully
addresses the significant impact plant closings have on individuals and
communities. And while we know that there is not unanimity of thought on
this topic we would like to continue the discussion of this most important
issue. Likewise, the City of Ithaca is concerned about job loss and has
provided funds to the Labor Coalition to do research into varying aspects
of job loss and plant closings.

Last year, the Ithaca Common Council affirmed its support of advance
notitication by voting to include in its lease agreements, with businesses
moving into the city industrial parks, a statement asking for 90 days advance
notice of intended closings or large layoffs. Also, last year the Governor
signed the New York State Compact with business and labor encouraging advance -
notification of intended closings or large layoffs. At all levels of avert
