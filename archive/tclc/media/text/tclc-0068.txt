In planning and performing our audit of the financial statements of the Mid-State Central Labor Council for the
year ended December 31, 1998, we considered the internal control structure to determine our auditing
procedures for the purpose of expressing an opinion on the financial statements and not to provide
assurance on the internal control structure.

However, during our audit, we became aware of matters that are an opportunity for strengthening operating
efficiencies. This letter summarizes our current year comments and recommendations regarding these
matters. This letter does not affect our report dated February 25, 1999.

CURRENT FINDINGS:

Payment of Invoices

: : Zz Finding:

= During our current year audit, we noted that the system of authorization for invoices was not being used for
all invoices. Payroll for hourly employees was not consistently authorized by the supervisor. Payments for
payroll were sometimes combined with expense reimbursements.

Recommendation:

We recommend that all payments of invoices and hourly payroll for employees be authorized and invoices
should be stamped with the check number and date of payment to indicate that they have been paid. All
reimbursement of expenses for employees should be done with a separate check.

Dues Receivable

Finding:
During our current year audit, we noted that the treasurer did not have sufficient information to know if all of
the per capita dues had been collected for the year.

Recommendation:

We recommend that a system be set up for the treasurer to know what dues have been paid and what dues
are still outstanding.

Organizational Identity

Finding:

During our current year audit, we noted that the identity of the Organization is not consistently stated. The old
Organization is The Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition, Inc., the new Organization is the Mid-State Central
Labor Council. Each Organization has their own federal identification number.

Recommendation:

We recommend that all bank accounts, payroll tax filings, income tax filings and contracts be in the name of
the Mid-State Central Labor Council and that the federal identification number being used should be the
number for the Labor Council. We recommend that if the Labor Council wants to use the Labor Coalition
name that they should file a doing business as form with the Tompkins County Clerk to make it clear that the
Organization is the Labor Council. If the Labor Council no longer wants to use the Labor Coalition name then
they should notify the Secretary of State that they want to dissolve the corporation.

We will review the status of these comments during our next audit engagement. We have already discussed
these comments and suggestions with the Council personnel, and will be pleased to discuss them in further
detail at your convenience, to perform any additional study of these matters, or to assist you in implementing
the recommendations.

This letter is intended for the information of the management of the Mid-State Central Labor Council and its
Steering Committee.
