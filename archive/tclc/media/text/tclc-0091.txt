Dear Labor Council Leaders:

Throughout 1996 the AFL-CIO has been engaged in a systematic outreach effort to its
Central Labor Councils. Key activities in this regard were:

= The creation of a national Labor Council Advisory Committee composed of 24
elected officers from large, medium and small sized councils from throughout the
nation.

The first ever national conference for all labor councils held in July in Denver,
Colorado. Representatives from nearly 150 labor councils attended this three day

meeting.

An intensive telephone survey of over 300 labor councils conducted by nearly 20
AFL-CIO field staff to ascertain the opinions of Council leaders on programs,
problems and the future of labor councils.

These efforts have engendered a process of rethinking and discussing the mission and
programs of the AFL-CIO Labor Councils and how, together, we can strategically align them with
the four major objectives of the AFL-CIO: Organizing, Mobilization, Effective Political and
Legislative Action and Coalition Building.

UNION CITIES

Equal Strong Communities

Strong, vibrant unions make Amencan cities and towns better places to live and
work. To help bning €conomuc security and justice to working families and a healthier
political and social environment to the community as a whole, the AFL-CIO Union Cities

program will provide a framework through Central Labor Councils to rebuild the labor
movement at its grassroots. }

Central Labor Councils will be challenged to bring local unions together around a
set of eight goals designed to build stronger unions, stronger labor councils and stronger -
communities, and then pass a resolution committing themselves to meet these goals.

Central Labor Councils that have committed to become Union Cities will be
announced in early 1997, and councils whose jurisdictions achieve Union City status will

be recognized annually thereafter.

The Union Cities resolution commits the CLC and its local unions to work toward
the following goals:

e Enrolling at least half of the local unions in the CLC in the AFL-CIO “Changing to
Organize” program, which guides local unions in coordination with their
international unions toward shifting at least 30 percent of their resources into
organizing, and adopting and implementing an organizing plan;

Recruiting and activating at least one percent of the total union membership in the
jurisdiction as part of a rapid response/solidarity team that can be mobilized to

support worker struggles in the community;

Reaching an overall membership growth rate in the jurisdiction of three percent
per year by the year 2000;

a Organizing grassroots legislatve/political action committees from the precinct
level up, with at least 100 union activists in each local and legislative district in the
council’s jurisdiction, and mobilizing members as well as community parmers;

SI Sponsoring the new AFL-CIO economucs education program for a majority of the
local unions in the jurisdiction;
