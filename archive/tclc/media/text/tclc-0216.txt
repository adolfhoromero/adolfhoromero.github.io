PROFILE ON SMITH CORONA LAYOFFS
Possible Violation of the Federal Plant Closing Law (WARN)

Background:

On March 16, 1990 Smith Corona Machine (SCM) announced it
was laying off 350 workers from its Cortland plant. The pink
slips were to be given out that weekend and the following week.

At the same time SCM announced that it was adding 300 jobs
to its plant in Singapore, due to the increased demand for
cheaper products made there.

The March layoff came on the heels of another SCM layoff in
late January of over 100 workers, bringing the total number of
workers laid of within about 60 days to more than 450.

According to laid off workers, some were called at home and
told not to report for work, others received notice when they
arrived at the plant and others were given a few days. It seems
clear that the vast majority of workers were not given even three
days notice before they were shown the door.

Needless to say the layoff took Tompkins and Cortland
Counties and the workers by surprise. There was no time for
contemplation or preparation. In fact, according to some workers
company spokespeople has expressly denied, only the previous
week, that any layoffs would occur.

After learning of the layoffs the Tompkins/Cortland Labor
Coalition (TCLC) decided to look into whether or not SCM had
violated the federal plant closing/mass layoff law. There were a
number of reasons for our decision to look into the matter.

First, the TCLC learned, from national labor sources, that a
Chicago law firm, Seyfarth, Shaw, Fairweather and Geralson, has
been training employers across the country how to avoid the
layoff thresholds which activate the WARN notification
requirements and that many employers are attempting to space
layoffs in such a way as to avoid providing the notification.

The TCLC decided that if SCM was simply trying to avoid the
notification requirements of WARN it would be important to
document. This law is being reviewed this year by Congress and
it will be important to document it's weaknesses and possible
violations with specific cases.

Secondly, the TCLC felt, based on available evidence (news
reports and discussions with workers), it is possible that SCM
violated WARN. Specifically, two sections of the law could have
been violated. The first would be Section 2(a)(2) which defines
a plant closing as "the permanent or temporary shutdown of :
one or more facilities or operating units within a single site of
employment .. . if [it] results in an employment loss .. . for
50 or more employees . .." The second would be Section 3 which
defines a mass layoff as "a reduction in [work] force which
results in an employment loss at the single site of employment

for .. .at least 33 percent of the employees." Neither of
these will be verifiable without an investigation and possibly a
lawsuit. (Smith Corona has no union representing its workers and
therefore access to company records is not possible.)

the layoffs are effecting workers, many of whom have been working

Fourth, the TCLC discussed the importance of addressing our
community's, and our country's, vulnerability to the "global

communities are being put in direct competition for jobs with
workers in countries where the wage levels are less than a third

SCM has established a plant in Singapore to take advantage
of cheap labor. Clearly, Cortland workers could produce SCM's
cheaper quality typewriters here if the machinery and equipment
had been put here. In 1985 SCM was granted "Free Trade Zone”
status by the federal government. This program is designed to
protect American jobs and while SCM's employment did go up
significantly from 1986 to 1989, they are clearly moving many of
their American operations to Singapore now. In light of this
fact it is important that we face this reality squarely and deal
with it. What is the future for Cortland, Tompkins County or the
hundreds of other communities like ours if we must compete with
third world working conditions.

For all of these reasons, on Monday April 2, 1990 the TCLC
joined with environmental activists (who have fought SCM on their
waste disposal practices)in a press conference to announce our
efforts to look into SCM's actions.

Did SCM Violate WARN?

The WARN 60-day advance notification requirement is
triggered in a number of ways. There are at least three which
may apply to the current SCM situation.

1. A plant closing means the permanent shutdown of one or
more operating units within a single site of employment when it
effects 50 or more workers. [Sec. 2(a)(2)]

2. A mass layoff means a reduction of at least 33% of the
workforce in any 30-day period. [Sec. 2(a)(3)]

3. A mass layoff also means separate layoffs totalling 33%
of the workforce in any 90-day period (unless the employer can
show that the separate layoffs were for separate and different
reasons). [Sec.3(d)]

Based on newspaper reports and information from workers who
have called the TCLC it seems possible that any one of these
three definitions may have been met.
