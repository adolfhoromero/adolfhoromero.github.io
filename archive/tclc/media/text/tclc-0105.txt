Re: Casualties

Date: April 28, 1995

The war between the Building Trades and the Labor Coali-
tion which, at this writing, seems to be escalating rather
than de-escalating, is not without real casualties. Since
these have not, to my knowledge, been explicitly pointed
out yet, I thought it would be important to do so.

1. Solidarity Ads
Last year the Building Trades members contributed $1,250

in ads to the Solidarity issue of the Working Press. This
obviously will be reduced this year. $450 from Laborers
and Masons is out already; we’ll probably lose another
$500 at least (anybody have other guesses?) for a loss of
$950.

2. Dues

Last year the Building Trades members paid $1,526 in dues.
I don’t know how much we have received so far this year.
We will obviously lose the $265 from the Laborers and
Masons, probably some on top of that from others. lLet’s
estimate the total loss at $500.

3. 1995 Health and Safety
The Trades received a $10,000 grant this year of which the

Labor Coalition is targeted to receive about $6,500 for
training programs, a needs assessment and technical assis-
tance. The program is largely on hold right now. What
happens for the duration of this year is hard to predict
at this point, but it is quite possible that the needs as-
sessment may not happen and technical assistance will be
less than planned. If so it means a loss of at least
$2,000 relative to what we budgeted as income for this

year.

4. 1996 Health and Safety
It is likely that there will be no Building Trades grant

for 1996, immediately lowering our "hoped-for" income for
next year by $5,000. In fact the loss may be much greater
for us (and them). Given the large membership in the

Building Trades unions and the very high risk involved in
construction work, the Building Trades’ potential for
health/safety funding is far higher than the $10,000

5. My own feelings of demoralization

Since I had long struggled to establish this program and
these ties with the Building Trades, and since it has been
a great success so far, it feels like a very significant
loss to me. I also feel some personal responsibility for
the conflict that has developed (in not acting more
Strongly to prevent it, as I think it should have been).
It also reinforces a sense that I have that the Coalition
leadership is leading but not enough members are follow-
ing; that our roots and ties with rank-and-file members
are increasingly weak and tenuous; and that this is not
compensated for by our increasingly strong ties with
community groups.

6. 1995 Budget
If my figures above are right - and they seem fairly

reasonable to me - we should immediately review the entire
1995 budget. Actually, the situation may be even worse
than this for two reasons. First, it is quite possible
that CNYOHCC will not have the same amount of outreach
funding available for 1995-1996 as this year, so we may
experience a cut in the latter half of this year compared
to what we currently receive. Second, it is quite possi-
ble that the UAW 1326 grant will not be fully carried out,
resulting in a potential reduction of our expected income

for 1995 of about $750. (Very little has been done during
