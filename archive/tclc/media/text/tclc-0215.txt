Smith Corona Corporation strongly supports the continuation
of the Foreign-Trade Zone Program as it exists today. It
considers its designation as Foreign Trade Subzone 930A a
significant factor in the extraordinary turnaround it has
achieved since July, 1985. Since that time Smith Corona has
increased production sixfold, more than doubled employment
and has initiated a highly successful export program. Smith
Corona believes that the continuation of the Foreign-Trade
Zone Program is instrumental to its efforts to maintain its
position as the leading manufacturer of portable electronic
typewriters and personal word processors in the globai

market.

First in Smith Corona's recovery plan was the redesign and
manufacture of a high quality, cost competitive typewriter.
In order to obtain the high quality components required to
achieve this goal, some of the components had to be foreign
sourced since they were not available in the domestic
market. Once the necessity to foreign source was

identified, Smith Corona discovered that, while it, an
American company trying to retain U.S. jobs had to pay

4 - 10 & duty on imported components, its foreign
competitors imported their fully assembled typewriters,
containing many of the same components (but no US labor
content) duty free. To rectify this situation and to enable
Smith Corona to purchase key components on a cost
competitive basis, Smith Corona applied for and was granted
(in a record four months) the privilege of cperating as 2
Foreign Trade Subzone. If Smith Corona's application had
not been handled so expeditiously, it is conceivable that
the grant might have been issued too late for it to help
Smith Corona maintain its operations in the United States.
