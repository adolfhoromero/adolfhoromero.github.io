GOALS AND OBJECTIVES

Our program is designed to reduce the incidence of work-
related injury and illness among the 15,000 unionized workers in
our 45 member unions and the 75,000 non-unionized workers in our
Central NY region (Tompkins, Cortland, Schuyler and Seneca
Counties). By educating and training workers to identify,
evaluate and protect themselves against workplace hazards, and by
helping form, train and improve the effectiveness of health and

safety committees, we seek to empower workers to reduce
workplace hazards and injuries.

Iv. Office and Clerical Workers

8. Health and Safety Newsletter for Office Workers

We plan to produce and distribute four issues, 2,750 copies
each, Of FYI: A Health and Safety Newsletter for Office Workers.
About half of the total copies are mailed or distributed to non-
union employees. It has proven to be extremely popular and a very
valuable and cost effective way to get critical health and safety
information to large numbers of workers, many of whom have no
union representation. In this way we can reach much larger
numbers of workers than we could with more traditional training
programs. The main objective of FYI is to educate office employ-
ees about work-related hazards, so they are better able to
identify the hazards, the ways they can be controlled, and other
sources of information and help.

FYI is not only a unique source of easily accessible and
usable health and safety information for office workers. It also
allows groups of office workers in disparate workplaces to
network among each other. The networking occurs not only because
information on different workplaces is included in FYI, but also
because it is produced through the collective effort of a number
of office workers, working with MCLC staff. Staff and volunteers
join to write and design each issue of FYI, using desktop pub-

lishing software.

Subject areas span the range of topics impacting on worker
safety and health. Future topics include hazard communication
requirements for office workers; VDTs and vision safety; build-
ing-related illness; multiple chemical sensitivity and others.

Office workers have no other source for the information
provided in this newsletter. Because of its uniqueness and
value, we will continue efforts to make FYI available to other
unions, COSH groups, occupational health clinics and other
organizations around the State that are interested in distribut-
ing it to groups of office and clerical workers.

10. Health and Safety Newsletter for School Employees

1 The four counties served by our central labor council
include about 15 unions representing approximately 3,500 K-12
school employees. There are, in addition, a large number of non-
unionized day care center and other school employees within our
jurisdiction. None of these school employees, whether union or
otherwise, have a regular source of information and education on
health and safety issues affecting themselves. This is our
objective in publishing FYI: A Health and Safety Newsletter for
School Employees.

With assistance from a volunteer group of school employees,
and using our extensive experience producing newsletters, we will
produce three issues of a four-page quarterly health and safety
newsletter for school employees. Working with the unions and
county day care councils, we will distribute this to 5,000 school
employees. Working with PTA. councils in the various school
districts, we will seek also to distribute this newsletter to
parents, a group that has a critical interest in school safety
and health, with an estimated 500 parents receiving it.

Most of the people getting this newsletter have very little
information relating to school health and safety, so this wali] be
a critical resource to them, and is the main objective in pub-
lishing it. The newsletter, in addition, will serve to link
school employees from all the districts and unions in our region,
thus providing a health and safety network that is sure to

provide important benefits in the future.

We have already produced three issues of this health and
safety newsletter (under our 1994-95 OSHTEP project). Topics
covered include indoor air quality, workplace violence, art and
craft hazards in the schools, carbon dioxide testing, pesticides,
and the high rate of breast cancer among teachers. Future topics
include: lab safety, radon, asbestos in schools, electromagnetic
fields, safety issues for school bus drivers.

Because MCLC already produces FYI, we have some infrastruc-

computer hardware, specifically a 486 or Pentium desktop computer
($1,500) and laser printer ($400). As of 1996 we will have 2.2

health and safety. The equipment we have access to, at this
point, is both shared with another organization as well as shared
with the non-health/safety work of the Labor Coalition. For
1996, as we increase our staff time and activities for health and
safety (the publication of the two FYI newsletters, in particu-

of this equipment increase their Own usage, the previous arrange-

health and safety training and education work funded under this
grant requires that we be able to purchase a computer and printer
as proposed in the draft contract.
