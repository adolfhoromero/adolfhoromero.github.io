Managerial Summary

Scope of Examination

We have examined the financial records and reports of the Tompkins Cortland
Labor Coalition for the period July 1, 1988 through June 30, 1990. The
general objectives of our examination were to determine whether the
financial statements of the auditee fairly present the cash position and
results of financial operations and whether the entity has complied with
laws and regulations that may have a material affect upon the financial
statements.

Background

This audit covers the contract agreements between NYSDOL and the Tompkins
Cortland Labor Coalition. NYSDOL reimbursed the auditee $69,487 to
administer this Occupational Safety and Health Training and Education
Program, Contracts No. 004972 and No. 005402 funded by the General Fund of
New York State. Reported expenditures for the audit period were $69,487.

Major Observations and Conclusions

Our audit disclosed that the auditee was in general compliance with
applicable State laws, regulations, and procedures. The financial reports
of the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition present fairly the results of
financial operations and cash position for the period examined except for
the following departures from prescribed State fiscal policy:

1. The auditee did not maintain a documented cost allocation plan to
support the distribution of certain joint expenses between programs.
This resulted in $3,747.75 of questioned costs.

2. The auditee incorrectly reported certain fringe benefit costs. This
resulted in questioned costs of $668.02.

3. The auditee incorrectly allocated rent. This resulted in $90.00 of
questioned costs.

4. The auditee did not provide a clear audit trail for reported program
costs. No questioned costs resulted from this finding.

The total questioned cost is $4,505.77.

It was determined that the findings outlined above did not materially
affect the financial reports submitted to NYSDOL.

SUMMARY

e have examined Schedules A and B which summarize reported cash position
and financial results of the Occupational Safety and Health Training and
Education Program (OSH), Contracts No. 004972 and No. 005402 for the period
July 1, 1988 through June 30, 1990. These contracts were awarded by the

New York State Department of Labor to the Tompkins Cortland Labor
Coalition.

SCOPE OF AUDIT

This report covers our examination of financial transactions, accounts and
reports for the period July 1, 1988 to June 30, 1990.

Our examination was made in accordance with the provisions of the Standards
for Audit of Governmental Organizations, Programs, Activities and

Functions, promulgated by the Comptroller General, which pertain to
financial and compliance audits.

Our examination included such tests of the accounting and program records
and such other auditing procedures as we considered necessary in the
circumstances. The aforementioned Schedules A and B are not intended to
present either the financial results of operations or financial position in
conformity with generally accepted accounting principles.

This audit covered all activities, functions, etc., which may have had a
material effect on the financial statements of the audited entity. The
audit of compliance management practices included the following areas:

- Financial Control and Accounting Systems
- Allowability of Costs
- Limitations on Costs

We have used judgmental sampling to test the audit universe as universe
sizes were too small to employ statistical sampling techniques. The total
expenditures for the period were $69,487. Our audit sample totalled
$21,174 or 31% of the total expenditures.

York State OSH Legislation and the auditee's contract agreements with
NYSDOL. It covered the period July 1, 1988 through June 30, 1990.

Cortland Labor Coalition to operate an Occupational Safety and Health
Training and Education Program. The goal of the program is to promote safe
and healthy conditions in the workplace. This is to be achieved through
training, education, and other proven preventive programs designed to:

The objective of internal accounting and administrative control is to
provide reasonable, but not absolute, assurance as to the safeguarding of
assets against loss from unauthorized use or disposition, and the
reliability of financial records for preparing financial statements and
maintaining accountability for assets. The concept of reasonable assurance
recognizes that the costs of a system of control should not exceed the
benefits derived and also recognizes that the evaluation of these factors
necessarily requires estimates and judgments by management.

There are inherent limitations that should be recognized in considering the
potential effectiveness of any system of internal and administrative
accounting control. In the performance of most control procedures, errors
can result from misunderstanding of instructions, mistakes of judgment,
carelessness or other personal factors. Control procedures whose
effectiveness depends upon segregation of duties can be circumvented by
collusion. Similarly, control procedures can be circumvented intentionally
by management with respect to the estimates and judgments required in the
preparation of financial statements.

Further, projection of any evaluation of control to future periods is
subject to the risk that the procedures may become inadequate because of
changes in conditions and that the degree of compliance with the procedures
may deteriorate.

Our study and. evaluation made for the limited purposes set forth in the
first paragraph of this report would not necessarily disclose all material
weaknesses in the system nor all deviations from prescribed compliance
requirements because it was based on selective tests of accounting records
and related data. Accordingly, we do not express an opinion on the system
of internal accounting control. However, our study and evaluation
disclosed no condition that we believe to be a material weakness.

Our audit disclosed that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition
incorrectly reported certain fringe benefit costs in Contract No.
005402. Based on our analysis of the actual fringe benefit billings,
we determined that FUTA and disability insurance were underreported and
health insurance was overreported. This resulted in questioned costs
of $668.02. See Auditor’s Exhibit I-B for details.

We recommend that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition report fringe
benefit costs based on actual expenditures per current policies and
billings. In addition, the fringe benefit costs reported should be
restricted to the wage allocation percentages of the staff.

Our audit disclosed that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition
incorrectly allocated rent. The rent expense for three months was
misallocated to the contract. This misallocation totalled $90.00 and
is questioned. See Auditor's Exhibit I-C for details.

We recommend that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition implement
procedures to ensure all costs are properly allocated and reported.

Our audit disclosed that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition did not

provide a clear audit trail for reported program expenditures. The
official books of entry were monthly financial reports which summarized
the entire agency’s checkbook activity. There were no worksheets

provided to reconcile the contract's expenses recorded on the official
books with the expenditures reported on the reimbursement vouchers
‘submitted to NYSDOL. Due to the lack of reconciliation procedures, it
is possible that not all program costs were reported.

Based on our analysis of the contract budget, payroll register and
invoices, we determined the costs reported on the reimbursement
vouchers to be reliable. As a result, the vouchers were used as the
basis for our audit. From our analysis of vouchered amounts, no
material discrepancies were disclosed.

We recommend that the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition develop and
implement controls and procedures that would provide a clear audit
trail from the official books of entry to the reimbursement vouchers
submitted to NYSDOL. This system should provide a reconciliation of
