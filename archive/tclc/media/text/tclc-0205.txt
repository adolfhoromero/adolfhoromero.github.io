History of Smith-Corona Corp. ("the Company") [SCO on NYSE]:

Co. incorporated in 1985 in Delaware . Until 1986,the businesses
of the Company were operated by SCM Corporation ( in the
chemical, paper, food business, as well as the businesses of the
Company) . On March 31,1986, SCM Corp. was acquired by Hanson Plc,
which thus owned the businesses of the Company through various
subsidiaries. The typewriter and word processing operations of
the Company were managed as an integrated business within this
structure. On Aug.3,1989, Hanson Plc reorganized the structure,
and the Smith Corona Corporation issued a stock offering for the
restructured Company, which combined the electronic typewriter,
personal word processor, and office supply businesses under the
single parent entity: Smith-Corona Corp. The office supply
business is carried on by two wholly-owned subsidiaries: SCM
Office Supplies and Histacount Corp. The only other major
products of the Company are the portable and compact electronic
typewriter lines and the line of personal word processors.

Hanson Plc is a holding co. with HQ in London.It is a highly
diversified conglomerate which employs 131,000. (St.& Poor’s 90)
Hanson Plc owns 46.3% of outstanding stock of Smith-Corona Corp.

Flexibility:

The company owns or leases space in Cortland, London, Toronto, &
Singapore (plus Ct.HQ). No mention of Mexican maquiladoras.
Co. owns 422,000 sq. ft. for manufacturing in Cortland, and
leases another 208,000 sq. ft. in Cortland for manufacturing &
warehousing.

The company leases land in Singapore from the government . Lease
expires April 30,2033. The company says the Singapore site 1s
used primarily for electronic typewriters and personal word
processor businesses. The company has "pioneer tax status" in
Singapore, and thus pays on taxes on unremitted Singapore

earnings.

Business Strategy:

Sales of personal word processors have been steadily increasing
(as a % of sales: 2.3% in 1987, 8.9% in 1988,16.2% in 1989),
while sales of portable and compact electronic typewriters have
been falling (61.9% in 1987,56.7% in 1988,52.6% in 1989).

Foreign sales have also been increasing faster than sales in US:

Net Sales: 1987 1988 1989
U.S. $334,981,000 $381,050,000 $479,069,000
Foreign $47,856,000 $79,609,000 $137,896,000

In 1989 Annual Report, Co. stated that it "may increase the

number of products that it manufactures abroad in the future."
The Co. indicated that "electronic reference products are
manufactured by independent contractors in the Far East." The
company wants to increase its sales in international markets.

It also notes that the typewriter/word processor business is
rapidly "maturing" with increasing price competition, which will
force it to cut product prices to avoid loss of market share. Thus
it is focusing on ways to cut manufacturing costs.

On Sept.5,1989, the company unveiled a new product: the PWP
7000LT laptop personal word processor, which sells for $899
including printer.

In the 1989 Annual Report, the Co. states:

"Today, we are a leading,global manufacturer and supplier of
portable and compact electronic typewriters, personal word
processors, and related accessories and supplies for use in the
home, school & office." The co, claimed that in 1989 it had
achieved "increased penetration of world markets."
