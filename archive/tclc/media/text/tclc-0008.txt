In each year there shall be a Legislative Conference to be held in the
City of Albany, or such other city within the State of New York, time
and place to be designated by the Executive Council.

The Participants in such conference, which shall be presided over by
the President, shall be the Executive Council and no more than three
(3) Tepresentatives of each affiliated body and such other persons as
may be invited by the Executive Council.

The Secretary-Treasurer shall give all affiliated bodies written notice
of the time and place of the Legislative Conference not less than thirty

(30) days prior to the day fixed for its meeting.

Upon the conclusion of the Conference, the Executive Council shall
hold a meeting and draw up and adopt with the assistance and advice
of the President, the policies and program of the organization to be
followed by the President in behalf of the organization at the ensuing

Legislative Session.

The President and the Secretary-Treasurer shall keep a record of the
action taken at the Legislative Conference and of the policies and
program of the organization as adopted by the Executive Council and
the President shall send copies thereof to all affiliated bodies.

held within the state of New York at a time and place designated by

the Executive Council.

The financial affairs of the organization shall be conducted and the
records and accounting of its funds kept and maintained in accord-
ance with the principles, standards, and rules promulgated in the
: inancial Practices and Proprietary Activities of Unions,” and in the

Minimum Accounting and Financial Controls,” both embodied in
Ethical Practices Code V approved by the Executive Council of the
AFL-CIO on May 22, 1957, and any additions and amendments
thereto or modifications thereof or any substitution therefor which
may at any time hereafter be adopted by said AFL-CIO.

The Executive Council shall meet upon the call of the President at
least two (2) times within each year, at a time and place, within the
State of New York, designated by the President. Special meetings of
the Council shall be held at the direction of the President with the
approval of the Secretary-Treasurer. Such special meetings shall deal
only with the subject or subjects specifically stated in the Call.

The Secretary-Treasurer shall give the members of the Council writ-
ten notice of the time and place of any meeting within a reasonable
time of such meeting, and in the case of a special meeting, shall
accompany the notice with a statement of the subject or subjects to
be submitted for consideration at the said meeting.

The Secretary-Treasurer shall keep minutes of all meetings of the
Council.

A majority of the members of the Executive Council shall constitute
a quorum for the conduct of its business.

Except as may otherwise be provided for in this Constitution,
decisions made by the Council and other actions taken, must be
concurred in by not less than a majority of the members of the Council

present and voting.

There shall be an Executive Committee composed of the President,
the Secretary-Treasurer, and up to fifteen (15) Vice-Presidents to
serve at the will of the Executive Council and to chosen by the

Executive Council.

The two (2) Executive Officers shall advise and consult with the
Executive Committee on policy matters.

If so ordered by the Executive Council, delegates whose credentials
have not been received by the Secretary-Treasurer at least five (5) days
before the time fixed for the convening of a convention shall not be
seated or permitted to participate as delegates. If the Executive
Council so orders, it may waive this requirement in special cases and
On good cause shown.

The Credentials Committee which shall be appointed by the Presi-
dent in consultation with the Executive Council, shall meet at least
one (1) day prior to the opening of the convention at an office
provided in the convention city. The Secretary-Treasurer shall certify
to said Committee the list of original credentials received by him and
the roll of delegates which he has prepared. He shall also advise said
Committee of the names of delegates representing organizations
which are in arrears,or whose right to a seat in the convention has
been protested. It shall be the duty of this Committee to pass on the
validity of the designation of delegates and their eligibility to serve as
such to hear and decide any contests or disputes that may arise with
respect to the designation of delegates or their eligibility, and to
prepare and certify to the convention a list of all the delegates duly
designated and empowered to participate in the work of the conven-

tion, and to vote thereat.

The decisions and other actions of the Committee on Credentials
shall be subject to appeal to the convention, but no person whose
right to be seated is involved by any such decision or action shall be
permitted to vote in the convention on an appeal therefrom.

After a convention has been called into session, the Committee on
Credentials shall submit at least a report of its work up to that time,
and any appeals therefrom shall be heard. No votes shall be taken by
a convention until this has been completed.

Alternate delegates may be certified to the convention in the same
manner as heretofore provided for delegates, but may be seated,
participate, and vote only in the absence of the delegate to which he
is an alternate, and on the certification of the Committee on Creden-
tials of the absence of the primary delegates.

Prior to the time fixed for the convening of a convention, and from
time to time during the course of its proceedings, the President, in
consultation with the Executive Council, and subject to the approval
of the convention, shall appoint such committees and officials as he
or the convention may deem necessary or desirable for the conduct
of the business of the convention. In no event shall any committee
appointed hereunder exceed fifty (50) members.
