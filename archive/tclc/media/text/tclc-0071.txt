Newfield Teachers Out On The Line

The Newfield Teachers’
Association (NTA) is standing
Strong in their struggle for a more
equitable contract with the New-
field School Board. After 17
months of working without a con-
tract, some progress has been
made but the School Board has
still not come up with a proposal
that addresses the major inequities
in the compensations system.

The teachers’ salaries in
the Newfield School District lag
far behind the salaries of other
Tompkins County teachers. In
fact, not only is the Newfield

teachers’ median salary the lowest
for any public school in Tompkins
County, it is ranked 574th out of
597 districts in New York State.
The District’s present
salary proposal is still just over 3%
in the next two years, lower than
the salary increases received by

other teachers in the County. At
this rate, the District will continue
to lose some of its best teachers to
districts that are willing to pay

Steelworkers Score Victory at U.S. Salt

The workers at U.S. Salt, members of Steelworkers, Local 12460 scored a major
victory on Halloween Eve, winning a new contract with their employer. The
Union was faced with management proposals full of Union givebacks on issues
centered around seniority, forced overtime, and discipline and prepared for a

strike.

The Labor Coalition, after hearing about the impending strike and U.S. Salt’s plan
to bring in replacement workers, planned a rally for Sunday, November 1. U.S.

Salt could see the writing on the wall - workers around Central NY standing to-
gether for a fair contract - and they gave in late Halloween eve.

What was going to be a picket line on November 1 turned into an inspired victory —
rally. Steelworkers were joined by the Labor Coalition staff and steering commit- _
tee, as well as UAW 2300, Odessa and Newfield Teachers, and the Building IL

Trades.

Community Mobilizes with Local Nurses

A group of registered nurses at the Cayuga Medical Center have recently
decided to organize a Union with the help of CSEA/AFSCME, Local
1000. Nurses are fighting inadequate staffing, extra-long shifts on short
notice, job redesign which assigns nursing duties to untrained hospital staff,
and the lack of ongoing continuing education for nurses.

CSEA asked the Cayuga Medical Center management to subscribe to a set
of principles, guaranteeing an organizing campaign free of intimidation and
threats. Cayuga Medical Center has made it clear that it has no intention
of subscribing to these principles. You can help.

—Huck/Konopacki, UAW-LUPA

Write a letter to Bonnie Howell, President and CEO of Cayuga Medical Center. This letter should state your
support for a Nurses’ Union, and should call on the hospital to subscribe to the “Principles of a Fair

Campaign”. Send the letter to: Cayuga Medical Center, 101 Dates Drive; Ithaca, NY 14850. Please send a
copy of your letter to CMC Nurses Association/CSEA; 622 W. State St.; Ithaca, NY 14850.

The nurses have already reached 3,000 community members who are supporting their organizing efforts.
