5/30/85
To the Editor;

A small, loose knit group of employees at Cornell refer to themselves
collectively as "Workers Against Unions." Using the sympathetic Ithaca
Journal as a forum, WAU has expressed a good deal of outrage toward the
United Auto Workers, the union which represents them on the job. while
much of their complaining is desultory and vague, there is one area in
which they are crystal clear: they see a Union Shop clause as an
infringement on their freedom of choice.

I try to avoid the argument which emphasizes the fact that all bar-
gaining unit members, whether or not they join the union, are by law
represented by officers freely elected by their fellow workers. In a
country where citizens feel increasingly alienated from their elected
leaders, cynical about the democratic process, and debilitated by a
general feeling of powerlessness, this argument tends to bring out
the closet monarchist in many. After all, why should I expect people
to want to pay dues and vote when so many pay taxes and still don't vote.

These "Workers Against Unions" simply aren't interested in on the
job democracy, a voice and a vote in the workplace - never were. They
didn't vote for the union and see no reason why they should join, pay
dues, vote or participate in any way. In all fairness to their anger
let's make a few observations from a slightly different perspective.

Before the union there was always a closed shop at Cornell. In
those days all workers were required to belong to Day Hall. To understand
this assertion just remember that if the union had lost their election -
by say a vote of 500 to 400 - the 400 workers who had voted for the union
would still not have been allowed to have one. This Cornell closed shop
was as closed as a shop can get; in this closed shop no worker had a
vote of any kind, and no representation either.

If the WAU feels that management i§ usually on the correct side of
labor/management issues then they should join the union and speak out
and vote accordingly. This gives management a voice in the union hall
while bringing dissent and diversity to the union. This is what democracy
is all about. Without dissent a union becomes a club of pro-union people,
a cult which sees all issues from the same narrow perspective - in other
words an institution greatly resembling management. Surely, this is not
the kind of representation that the employees in WAU deserve.

I i
Jim Morrissette, Chair
Tompkins-Cortland Labor Coalition
