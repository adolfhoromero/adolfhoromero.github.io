Collective Power from the 1980s to Now

  • WhenNov 16, 2026
  • RoleFeatured researcher
  • WhereCornell Graduate School · 250th Anniversary Research Spotlights

What happened

The Cornell Graduate School featured my research in its 250th Anniversary Research Spotlights, a series that invites graduate students and alumni to show how their work looks at the past and present of American life as the country turns 250. My spotlight covers the research behind my thesis, “Still a Union Town?”: how workers in Tompkins County have built, lost, and rebuilt collective power from the 1980s to today, traced through archives, labor newspapers, and oral histories.

In the 1980s the Tompkins Cortland Labor Coalition brought together more than 40 unions and over 10,000 workers in Tompkins and Cortland counties. When Ithaca Gun, Smith Corona, and the Cargill salt mine cut union jobs, the coalition lost much of its formal power. What it built stayed in the community: relationships, practical know-how, traditions like the annual Labor Day picnic, and its records. Many of the same organizers carried that into new groups. The Workers’ Center was one of them, and in 2006 it launched a Living Wage Employer Certification Program. Certified businesses put an emblem in the window so people in town could see which employers paid a living wage.

The piece also gets into why this matters now. Low-wage, immigrant, undocumented, and guest workers often have rights on paper that are hard to use in practice, and those gaps grow when agencies like the National Labor Relations Board are weakened. Local organizing is often what makes those rights real.

Thanks to the Graduate School for including me in the series, and to everyone who sent a note after it went up.

The coalition’s records are becoming a public archive on this site. You can follow that work on the TCLC archive project page or browse the archive.
